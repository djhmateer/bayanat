# Workflow Story — Lewes Castle Investigation
### Ouse Valley Rights Monitor

_Narrative account of how the documentation in this Bayanat instance was built. Written from the perspective of the documenting team — **Ouse Valley Rights Monitor**, a fictional small regional human rights documentation NGO. All names, organisations, and events are fictional._

---

## Background

On 17 March 2026 a crowd of approximately 200 people forced entry into Lewes Castle, occupying the grounds for three days. Seven arrests were made on 19 March when police cleared the site. The same afternoon The National Courier named Thomas Ashdown — one of those arrested — as "one of the organisers" of the occupation.

Ouse Valley Rights Monitor initiated an investigation on 22 March 2026, three days after the siege ended, once it became clear that criminal charges had been filed and that a structured evidence record was needed. A third incident — criminal damage to construction equipment at the Meridian House demolition site — was discovered on 24 March, two days into the investigation, when the team identified a Lewes & Weald Constabulary press release reporting overnight vandalism at the site whose planned demolition had triggered the original occupation.

### Bulletins _(individual pieces of evidence — raw source documents)_

Each bulletin is linked to an incident with two attributes:

- **Relationship type** — what role the evidence plays:
  - **Primary Evidence** — directly documents the incident (e.g. a photograph of the event, an official arrest statement)
  - **Supporting Evidence** — corroborates the primary evidence but is not definitive on its own (e.g. a news article reporting what police confirmed)
  - **Context** — not evidence of the incident itself, but relevant background (e.g. the Lewes Clarion article establishing motive)
- **Probability** — the analyst's confidence that the link is correct: _Certain_, _Likely_, _Possible_, or _Unlikely_

## Day 1 of the investigation — 22 March 2026

### Step 1 — Establishing context: the pre-incident bulletin

The team's first act was to document the trigger for the occupation. A Lewes Clarion article from 3 March 2026 reported that Lewes District Council had voted 7–4 to approve demolition of Meridian House, a Grade II listed building adjacent to the castle. The article quoted heritage campaign group Lewes Heritage Trust warning that "direct action will follow". This bulletin (**Bulletin 1**, assigned to Analyst One) was linked to Incident 1 with an itob relationship of **Context, Certain** — meaning it is not direct evidence of the incident itself, but is definitively relevant background establishing motive and the group's stated intent.

The Lewes Clarion bulletin was given a **Pre-Incident** event: _Council approves demolition of Meridian House_ (3 March 2026). This placed it correctly in the timeline even though it predated the occupation by two weeks. The event type **Incident** was not used here because the council vote was not itself a violation or harmful act — it was the trigger that preceded one. Using Pre-Incident preserves the causal narrative without mischaracterising the planning decision as part of the incident proper.

### Step 2 — Social media sweep: day 1 bulletins

Working through social media posted on 17 March, the team created four bulletins documenting the initial crowd at the castle:

- **Bulletin 2** (X post, 14:22) — photograph of the crowd outside the barbican gate. Assigned to Analyst Two.
- **Bulletin 3** (Facebook, 14:35) — eyewitness caption describing the crowd. Assigned to Analyst Three.
- **Bulletin 4** (Instagram, 14:41) — photograph taken from elevated position. Assigned to Analyst Three.
- **Bulletin 5** (TikTok, 15:04) — video footage from inside the castle grounds. Assigned to Analyst One.

Each bulletin was tagged, labelled, and linked to the **Lewes Castle** location. Bulletins 2–4 were given an **Incident** event (_Crowd forces entry through barbican gate_, 17 March 14:00–14:30). 

### Step 3 — Actor identification

The X post (Bulletin 2) contained a clear photograph of the crowd. Analyst Two reviewed the image and identified a man at the front of the crowd carrying a placard and using a megaphone. Cross-referencing with the National Courier article of 19 March — which named **Thomas Ashdown** as one of the organisers — the team was confident in the identification.

An Actor record was created for **Thomas Ashdown** (ID: 1):

- Full name: Thomas James Ashdown, nickname Tom
- Occupation: Self-employed builder, Lewes resident
- Position: Organiser, Lewes Heritage Trust
- Nationality: United Kingdom
- ID numbers: Crime Reference Number `CRN/2026/LEW/00312`, Crown Court Case Number `T20261234`
- Actor status: Assigned (to Analyst Two)

The actor profile (mode: Main) was populated with a full narrative description drawn from the X post, the eyewitness statement (Margaret Okafor), and the National Courier article. Sources linked: X (Twitter) and The National Courier.

Actor events were added:
- **Arrested** — Lewes Castle — 19 March 07:30–08:00
- **Charged** — Lewes Police Station — 19 March 15:30 (violent disorder, aggravated trespass)

## Day 2 of the investigation — 23 March 2026

### Step 4 — Day 2 and day 3 bulletins

Analyst Three and Analyst One continued the documentation sweep, creating bulletins for the second and third days of the siege:

- **Bulletin 6** (Eyewitness statement, Margaret Okafor, 18 March) — Okafor confirmed a man matching Ashdown's description was near the barbican gate at the time of the breach. Assigned to Analyst Two. Given a **Post-Incident** event (_Police begin formal negotiations_, 18 March 09:00–18:00).
- **Bulletin 7** (Albion Broadcasting, 18 March 17:45) — national broadcaster coverage of day 2. Noted Dr. Caroline Voss (site curator) had issued a statement via the National Monuments Authority describing structural damage to the Norman keep. Assigned to Analyst Three.
- **Bulletin 8** (Lewes & Weald Constabulary press statement, 18 March 12:00) — official police statement confirming the major incident, Section 14 dispersal zone, and ongoing negotiations. Assigned to Analyst One.
- **Bulletin 9** (The National Courier, 19 March 14:20) — end-of-siege article. Named Ashdown by name as an organiser. Seven arrests confirmed. National Monuments Authority confirmed the castle would remain closed for structural survey. Assigned to Analyst Two.
- **Bulletin 10** (Lewes & Weald Constabulary, 19 March 13:30) — arrest statement confirming seven people charged. Assigned to Analyst Three.

### Step 5 — Incident 1 created

With the core evidence in place, **Incident 1** was created:

Assigned to **Analyst Two**. Peer reviewers: Analyst Three (first), Analyst One (second). Status: **Assigned**.

Three incident events were linked:

All ten bulletins were linked with typed itob relationships (Primary Evidence, Supporting Evidence, Context) and probability scores. The National Courier article (Bulletin 9) and the X post (Bulletin 2) were marked **Primary Evidence, Certain**.

## Day 3 of the investigation — 24 March 2026

### Step 7 — Phoenix Causeway incident discovered

While reviewing the Albion Broadcasting day 2 article (Bulletin 7), Analyst Three noticed a paragraph mentioning a secondary group of supporters who had gathered at a Tesco superstore on Phoenix Causeway, Lewes, during the afternoon of 18 March, blocking loading bays before dispersing following a Lewes & Weald Constabulary Section 35 order. No arrests were made at that location.

An X post (Bulletin 11) and a further Albion Broadcasting article (Bulletin 12) documented this secondary protest. Both were created and linked to the new incident.

### Step 8 — Incident 2 created

**Incident 2** was created:

> **Unlawful assembly and obstruction — Phoenix Causeway, Lewes, 18 March 2026**

Assigned to **Analyst One**. Peer reviewers: Analyst Two (first). Status: **Assigned**.

One incident event: _Protest group occupies Tesco car park, Phoenix Causeway_ — Incident type — 18 March 13:00–17:00.

Bulletins 11 and 12 linked as Primary Evidence and Supporting Evidence respectively.

Potential violations: Public Order Offence, Obstruction of a Public Place.

### Step 9 — Incidents linked via itoi

The two incidents were connected via an **itoi relationship**:

> Incident 1 → **Related** → Incident 2 (probability: Likely)

Comment: _Tesco protest occurred on day 2 of the siege — same group, same day._

This relationship reflects the analytical judgment that both incidents were part of the same coordinated action by the Lewes Heritage Trust, without folding them into a single incident (the locations, legal character, and police response were distinct).

## Day 3 continued — Incident 3 discovered

### Step 10 — Criminal damage at the Meridian House site

While reviewing open sources on the morning of 24 March, Analyst Three found a Lewes & Weald Constabulary press release dated 22 March reporting criminal damage at the Meridian House demolition site — the very building whose approved demolition had triggered the castle occupation. In the early hours of 22 March, persons unknown had entered the Harvey's Brewery site, slashed the tyres of two excavators, and spray-painted protest slogans ("Heritage Not Rubble", "Meridian Lives") on site hoarding. Crucially, CCTV cameras had been obscured with spray paint before the damage — indicating premeditation and knowledge of the site layout.

A Lewes Clarion article (22 March, 13:45) corroborated the police account and added that the lead contractor, Apex Build Group, estimated repair costs at approximately £18,000.

**Incident 3** was created:

> **Criminal damage to construction equipment — Meridian House site, Lewes, 22 March 2026**

Assigned to **Analyst One**. Peer reviewers: Analyst Three (first), Analyst Two (second). Status: **Assigned**.

Two bulletins linked:
- Bulletin 13 (Lewes & Weald Constabulary press release) — Primary Evidence, Certain
- Bulletin 14 (Lewes Clarion) — Supporting Evidence, Likely

Potential violation: Criminal Damage. No claimed violations (no arrests, suspects unknown).

Both bulletins carry a geo marker at the Meridian House site coordinates (Harvey's Brewery site, `POINT(0.01664 50.87484)`).

### Step 11 — itoi: Incident 1 Led to Incident 3

A second itoi relationship was created:

> Incident 1 → **Led to** → Incident 3 (probability: Likely)

Comment: _Castle siege publicly identified the Meridian House site as the target; damage followed three days after clearance._

This is a stronger analytical claim than "Related" — the castle occupation put Meridian House on the public agenda and the damage appears to be a direct continuation of the campaign. It is recorded as Likely rather than Certain because no group has claimed responsibility and no individual has been charged.

**Thomas Ashdown is not linked to Incident 3.** He was on bail at the time and no evidence places him at the site. Incident 3's perpetrators remain unknown.

The investigation is in its early **Assigned** phase. The expected next steps are:

3. The team determines whether Ashdown should also be linked to Incident 2 (the Tesco protest) — currently he is only linked to Incident 1, as no direct evidence places him at Phoenix Causeway
4. Incident 3 remains in early triage — if an arrest is made, a new actor record will be created and linked
5. Any additional witnesses or suspects identified during criminal proceedings may result in new actor records

### Key analytical judgements made

- **Incident naming** follows IHRL methodology — neutral and descriptive, not media/narrative names ("Lewes Castle siege" was not used)
- **Three incidents** rather than one or two — each is geographically, legally, and operationally distinct; itoi relationships carry the connections
- **Ashdown linked to Incident 1 only** — no evidence places him at the Tesco protest or the Meridian House site; the link to Incidents 2 and 3 is indirect (same campaign)
- **Incident 1 → Led to → Incident 3** rather than "Related" — a deliberate analytical call reflecting the likely causal chain, recorded as Likely not Certain
- **Bulletin 1 (Lewes Clarion) linked as Context, Certain** — the itob relationship type "Context" reflects that it is not direct evidence of the incident but is definitively relevant to establishing motive and the group's stated intent
